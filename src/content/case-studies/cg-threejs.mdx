## What it is

A virtual pet inside a cardboard-style device with three buttons. It starts as an egg, hatches after 16 seconds (a riser sound starts 6 seconds before), and then lives in a small furnished room. The buttons make it eat, play or sleep. Shaking the scene knocks it over into a physics ragdoll. With the microphone on, it listens, and when you speak it repeats you back in a higher voice.

## A pet built from curves

The character has no imported model. The head and body are `LatheGeometry`: a cubic Bézier curve in 2D, spun around an axis. The ears are capsules on pivots, and the hands and legs reuse the body shape at smaller scales. The face is a texture I drew, swapped by state: happy, eating, talking (open and closed) and dizzy for the ragdoll.

The device is built the same way. It is a box with a smaller box subtracted from it using constructive solid geometry (`three-csg-ts`), which leaves the hollow room the pet lives in.

Idle motion comes from layered sine waves rather than keyframes. Each ear swings on two sines of different speed and size, the body rises and falls with a breathing cycle, and the head bobs slightly.

## The ragdoll handoff

The interesting moment is the switch from animation to physics. An animated character has no physical velocity: it is moved by code. If the ragdoll bodies are created at rest, the pet stops dead and then falls, which looks wrong.

So the pet measures its own velocity every frame, from how far its world position moved since the previous one. When the ragdoll starts, each new rigid body is created at the pose of the mesh it replaces and seeded with that velocity, before a random impulse knocks it over.

The ragdoll is three bodies: a capsule for the torso and two balls for the hands, joined at the shoulders with spherical joints. The hands have strong angular damping, added in a later commit for stability. After five seconds the bodies are removed, the meshes are put back in their rest pose and the walking capsule is recreated.

## Listening

With the microphone on, an analyser reads the input every frame and computes its RMS level. Above a threshold, and at most once every 1.2 seconds, it records a 1.4-second clip, then plays it back at 1.8× speed, which raises the pitch.

<CodeWindow file="js/Model/App.js" lang="js" href="https://github.com/Gabriel-S-Paiva/cg-threejs/blob/b37f1b3a15a15e9cdaf7a2e0f7d0c096f55dc4bb/js/Model/App.js#L264-L280" caption="The voice trigger: RMS over the time-domain samples, a threshold and a cooldown." code={micTrigger} />

The README calls the mouth animation lip-sync. To be exact: while the clip plays, the face toggles between the open and closed mouth textures six times a second, for the length of the playback. It follows the duration, not the loudness. Driving it from the same RMS value as the trigger would be the honest version of lip-sync.

## Three cameras

Keys 1, 2 and 3 switch between the fixed scene view, first person through the pet's eyes and a third-person follow camera. In the last two, WASD walks the pet, which uses a Rapier capsule with locked rotation so it collides with the furniture and walls.
