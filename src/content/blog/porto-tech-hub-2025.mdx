## The talk I picked

From the main track I went to *The Zen of Programming*, a Tuesday afternoon talk from a developer with four decades of experience who now works as a CTO. It was billed as a personal journey through languages, paradigms and principles, asking the questions every developer ends up asking at some point. Is there a silver bullet? How solid is SOLID? Why DRY? Is small beautiful? Does unit testing make debugging obsolete? The angle was what beautiful code means, and whether the answer changes from one developer to the next.

## Every single table

We went to every single table in the room. That's the part I'd recommend most. You can't know in advance which conversation will be the good one.

We ended up getting closer with the ITDS team, and there are photos of us with them on their page.

## The closing ceremony

I stayed for the closing ceremony too.

## This year is different

The 2026 edition is on 10 November. If I get a ticket again, I'll approach it differently. Last year a curricular internship wasn't an option for me yet, so my approach was the same as the day itself: look around and see what's out there. This year I can, and I have to, do one, so I'll be a lot more internship-oriented about who I talk to and what I ask.

If I go, I'll write that one up too.

## If you get a ticket

- Go to the talks, but don't skip the tables.
- Visit every one, even the ones that don't look relevant.
- Stay to the end.
